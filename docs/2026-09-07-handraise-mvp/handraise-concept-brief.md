# Handraise — A Volunteering and Help Coordination Network

## Concept

People often need concrete help but do not know whom they can safely ask. At the same time, many people are willing to contribute their time and abilities but lack a trusted way to discover where their help would be useful.

An elderly person may need a small repair at home. Someone recovering from illness may need an authorized person to administer prescribed medicine. An animal shelter may need help on-site. A festival, community project, or social institution may need several volunteers for specific shifts. A person may simply want to make themselves available to help others.

**Handraise** is a help coordination network that connects people and mission-led organizations with people who freely choose to help. It makes needs understandable, relevant opportunities discoverable, commitments explicit, and participation more transparent for everyone involved.

Handraise is centered on **volunteering**: help is freely chosen and unpaid. A person can decline before committing without penalty, and receiving help creates no debt, credit, or obligation to provide help later. Direct expenses, such as travel or materials, may be reimbursed when both parties agree to them transparently.

Handraise does not trade labor, assign volunteers, or decide who must help whom. It provides the structure needed for people to find one another, understand the situation, assess trust and safety, and make their own decisions.

## Core Principles

### Participation is always voluntary

Every act of help begins with a person's own choice. Browsing a request, expressing interest, or publishing availability is not a commitment. An engagement exists only after both parties explicitly confirm it.

Declining or withdrawing before mutual confirmation is neutral. After confirmation, either party may still withdraw, but cancellations and no-shows become part of the engagement history so that future participants have useful context.

### Help is unpaid

The MVP supports unpaid help only. Reimbursement of direct expenses is not compensation: the expense categories, responsibility, and a maximum amount or calculation method must be agreed before an engagement is confirmed. Changes require renewed consent from both parties.

Paid work, barter, time credits, and reciprocal obligations are outside the MVP. The underlying model should remain extensible enough to support other explicitly defined forms of exchange in future versions.

### Roles belong to interactions, not identities

One person may request help today and offer help tomorrow. Handraise therefore has no permanent requester-only or helper-only account types.

- **Requester** — a person or eligible organization seeking help in a particular situation.
- **Helper** — a person considering or providing help in a particular situation.
- **Help Request** — a structured description of a need, its context, timing, location, expectations, and relevant risks.
- **Availability Offer** — a non-binding description of what, where, and when a person is willing to help.
- **Engagement** — a mutually confirmed agreement between a Requester and a Helper for a specific Help Request.

### AI proposes; people decide

AI may turn an informal description into a clear Help Request, identify missing information, suggest categories and matching attributes, and flag possible safety, policy, or exploitation concerns.

The Requester approves the structured request before publication. Flagged requests may require human moderation. AI never publishes or rejects a request, changes a person's public profile, or commits someone to an engagement on their behalf.

### Trust is reciprocal and contextual

Helpers need to understand who is asking, what is expected, and whether previous requests were clear and respectful. Requesters need to understand a potential Helper's identity, capabilities, relevant history, and reliability.

Handraise therefore treats trust as mutual. It shows factual engagement history and structured feedback about clarity, respect, communication, reliability, safety, and outcome. It does not reduce a person to one opaque score.

### Privacy is purpose-bound

Handraise privately verifies individual identities, but users control what identity and personal context appear publicly. Precise addresses, fuller identity details, and sensitive need information are disclosed only when they are necessary for a specific engagement.

Private matching preferences, excluded activities, and personal constraints are used to improve recommendations but are not exposed to other users.

## Who Can Participate

### Individuals

An individual can both publish Help Requests and make Availability Offers through one profile. The profile may include:

- public identity information chosen by the person;
- interests, causes, and preferred forms of contribution;
- self-declared capabilities, experience, tools, languages, and availability;
- user-uploaded certificates, licenses, or other supporting documents;
- user-controlled context about personal needs;
- factual history of requests and engagements;
- structured mutual feedback.

Every claim must clearly identify its source and verification status. User-uploaded evidence is not described as verified unless Handraise has actually verified it.

AI may suggest profile updates based on conversation or repeated activity, but the person must confirm or edit a capability before it becomes public. Factual engagement history remains separate from self-declared or inferred capability claims.

### Mission-led organizations

Nonprofits, shelters, community groups, public institutions, social institutions, and other mission-led organizations may request help when the specific request serves a public or community benefit and does not replace routine paid staffing.

Legal form alone does not determine eligibility. Handraise applies a purpose-and-beneficiary test and validates an organization before its first Help Request can be published. Later requests continue to pass automated purpose and anti-exploitation screening.

Commercial operations cannot use Handraise to fill ordinary unpaid positions. A festival, social enterprise, or similar organization may qualify only when the particular request genuinely serves an eligible mission and is not disguised staff substitution.

## How Coordination Works

### 1. A need or offer is published

A Requester describes a need in ordinary language. AI proposes a structured Help Request containing the scope, desired outcome, location area, schedule, effort, roles, capacity, direct expenses, relevant capabilities, physical conditions, and safety considerations. The Requester reviews and approves it.

Alternatively, a person publishes an Availability Offer describing the kinds of help they are open to, their location or travel area, availability, preferences, and relevant capabilities. An Availability Offer is an invitation to connect, not a standing obligation.

### 2. People discover a suitable match

Helpers can browse and search Help Requests or receive relevant notifications. Requesters can discover Availability Offers and public profiles.

Recommendations prioritize genuine fit: interests, capabilities, availability, distance, private preferences, and safety requirements. Urgency is shown clearly as context, but it does not override fit or pressure someone to participate.

Unsolicited direct messages are restricted. Communication opens in the context of a Help Request or an inquiry based on an Availability Offer, keeping conversations purposeful and reducing spam.

### 3. Both parties clarify the engagement

A Helper may express interest without becoming committed. The parties can then clarify:

- the exact scope and desired outcome;
- date, time, duration, and location;
- roles and responsibilities;
- tools, materials, access, and working conditions;
- safety concerns and relevant capability claims;
- reimbursable expense categories and limits;
- how either person should communicate a change.

Only explicit confirmation by both parties creates an Engagement. A material change to scope, schedule, safety conditions, or expense terms requires renewed consent.

### 4. The engagement is carried out transparently

The lifecycle remains small and understandable: interest, mutual confirmation, start, completion, withdrawal, cancellation, no-show, or expiry. Each transition records who acted, when it happened, and relevant context.

A Helper can report that help was completed, partially completed, postponed, or could not be completed. The Requester can confirm what happened and provide structured feedback. The Helper can likewise provide feedback about the accuracy of the request, conditions, communication, safety, and respect.

### 5. The community record grows

Completed engagements build factual history for both participants. Patterns matter more than isolated incidents. Declining before commitment remains neutral, while repeated post-commitment cancellations, no-shows, unsafe conditions, unclear requests, or disrespectful conduct become visible in appropriate context.

## Group Volunteering

A Help Request may require multiple Helpers, distinct roles, or several shifts. For example, an animal shelter may need six Helpers across two time slots, while a festival may need setup, accessibility support, and cleanup roles.

The request defines roles, shift times, and capacity. Each Helper confirms an individual Engagement and receives an independently recorded outcome. One person's cancellation or no-show does not distort the history of everyone else in the group.

## Safety and Boundaries

### Prohibited requests

The MVP does not permit:

- illegal, deceptive, or deliberately harmful activity;
- emergency or crisis response, where users must be directed to appropriate emergency or professional services;
- sexual or intimate services;
- direct care involving children or vulnerable adults;
- requests that violate another person's rights or consent;
- routine commercial labor presented as volunteering.

### Safety-sensitive and regulated help

Some permitted requests may still involve meaningful risk or regulated activity. Handraise highlights those risks and requires explicit acknowledgement rather than silently treating them like ordinary tasks.

Handraise does not certify professional competence in the MVP. Helpers declare their relevant capabilities, and Requesters decide whether those claims are sufficient. Both parties must attest to safety-sensitive commitments before confirmation.

For a simple invasive medical procedure such as an intramuscular injection, the Helper must upload evidence that they are officially authorized to perform it. Handraise labels the evidence as user-provided and unverified. The Requester evaluates it, and both parties explicitly acknowledge the procedure and authorization claim before confirming the Engagement.

### Preventing exploitation

Freely given help can still become exploitative when it is repeatedly expected, used to replace paid staff, or expanded beyond the agreed scope.

Handraise tracks cumulative patterns of substantial help between participants, makes that history visible to them, and prompts them to review boundaries. Suspected routine staffing or exploitation is flagged for moderation. The MVP does not impose an automatic hour or frequency cap, because repetition alone does not determine whether participation is freely chosen.

## Illustrative Use Cases

### Home repair for an elderly person

An elderly Requester describes a leaking kitchen tap and limited mobility. Handraise structures the scope, required tools, location area, timing, and any material expenses. Nearby Helpers who have declared relevant experience can express interest. Both parties agree on the repair and reimbursement limit before confirming.

### An authorized injection at home

A person needs a prescribed intramuscular injection but cannot easily travel. The request is marked safety-sensitive. A Helper uploads evidence of official authorization, the Requester evaluates it, and both acknowledge the risk and claim before confirming. The request is not treated as emergency care.

### Animal shelter support

A validated shelter publishes a request for several Helpers to clean enclosures and organize supplies. It defines shifts, physical conditions, clothing requirements, and capacity. Each Helper confirms and completes an individual Engagement.

### Festival or community event

An eligible mission-led festival requests Helpers for setup, visitor guidance, accessibility support, and cleanup. Organization validation and request screening ensure that the roles serve the public mission rather than replace routine commercial staffing.

### Social institution support

A social institution requests help sorting donations and preparing a community space. Helpers discover the request based on location, availability, interests, and comfort with the environment.

### A person offering help

A person publishes an Availability Offer for minor repairs and event setup on weekends within a chosen travel radius. Requesters can discover the offer and open a request-based conversation. The person remains free to decline every inquiry.

## Suggested MVP

The MVP should validate whether Handraise can produce safe, well-understood, mutually confirmed help engagements in one pilot community.

1. One account and profile with contextual Requester and Helper roles.
2. Private individual identity verification and organization validation before first publication.
3. Progressive profile creation with interests, capabilities, privacy controls, and user-confirmed AI suggestions.
4. AI-assisted Help Request creation with Requester approval and flag-based human moderation.
5. Availability Offers for people who want to advertise when and how they are willing to help.
6. Fit-based feed, search, notifications, and request-linked communication.
7. Mutual confirmation after scope, schedule, safety, and expense clarification.
8. A compact engagement lifecycle with transparent history and neutral pre-commitment refusal.
9. Basic group roles, shifts, and capacity with individual Engagement tracking.
10. Structured mutual feedback and factual contribution history.
11. Safety warnings, user-provided authorization evidence, bilateral attestations, prohibited-category controls, reporting, and moderation.
12. Detection and review of repeated-help and suspected staff-substitution patterns.

## Future Extensions

The core model should allow later versions to introduce additional exchange types without weakening the clarity of the MVP. Possible extensions include paid or reciprocal arrangements as explicitly separate engagement models, platform-verified credentials, expanded safeguarding workflows, support for additional care categories, richer organization administration, and transparent explanations of matching recommendations.

These possibilities do not change the MVP commitment: Handraise begins as a network for freely chosen, unpaid volunteering with transparent expense reimbursement and no obligation to reciprocate.

## Questions for the Later PRD

The product requirements phase should convert this concept into explicit policy and acceptance criteria, including:

- the pilot location and applicable legal context;
- identity verification methods and disclosure rules;
- the evidence required to validate mission-led organizations;
- request screening thresholds and moderator workflows;
- the exact taxonomy of prohibited and safety-sensitive requests;
- the bilateral attestation and evidence-upload flow;
- lifecycle states and transition permissions;
- what feedback and history are public, private, or moderator-only;
- thresholds for repeated-help and staff-substitution review;
- notification, privacy, retention, appeal, and reporting policies;
- success measures for useful matches, completed help, safety, trust, and participant autonomy.
